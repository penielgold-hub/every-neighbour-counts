**Real People. Real Help. Measurable Impact.**

> Your family first. Your neighbour next. Nobody left behind.

## 1. Purpose

The Education Support Programme exists to help children and young people remain in education and progress to the next stage of their learning when financial hardship threatens their schooling or access to examinations.

The programme focuses primarily on pupils and students attending public primary and secondary schools, and eligible school-leavers seeking to sit recognised examinations or pursue further education.

We believe that a family's financial difficulty should not unnecessarily prevent a child from learning, completing an academic year, sitting an important examination or pursuing a realistic educational opportunity.

This programme is part of EVERY NEIGHBOUR COUNTS. It is an independent, volunteer-led community initiative. It is not a government scheme, school authority, examination body or promise of guaranteed financial assistance.

## 2. Objectives

The programme aims to:

- Identify pupils and students whose education is at risk because of financial hardship.
- Provide or mobilise support for verified, necessary education-related expenses.
- Help eligible students access recognised examination and admission processes.
- Connect families with scholarships, bursaries, school welfare support and other legitimate opportunities.
- Encourage families, neighbours, faith-based organisations, community groups, businesses and individual supporters to contribute to educational access.
- Record assistance and outcomes responsibly so that the community can understand what support has achieved.

## 3. Who the programme may support

Priority may be given to:

- Pupils in public primary schools.
- Students in public junior and senior secondary schools.
- Students at risk of missing school or withdrawing because of an inability to meet essential education costs.
- Eligible candidates preparing for WAEC, NECO or other recognised examinations.
- Eligible secondary-school leavers seeking support with JAMB/UTME registration and related admission steps.
- Children and young people facing additional barriers, including those living with disabilities, orphanhood, displacement or serious household hardship.

Support is based on demonstrated need, available resources and the programme's capacity. No applicant is automatically entitled to assistance.

The programme may consider exceptional cases outside public schools where there is a clear reason and resources permit, but its initial priority is public-school pupils and students.

## 4. Types of support

Depending on available resources and verified needs, assistance may include:

### 4.1 Primary education
- Essential textbooks, exercise books and learning materials.
- School uniforms, footwear and basic school supplies.
- Verified, necessary school charges or levies.
- Other practical items required for a child to attend and participate in school.

### 4.2 Secondary education
- Verified school fees, approved levies or essential charges where applicable.
- Textbooks, notebooks, stationery and learning materials.
- Uniforms and other basic school necessities.
- Support to prevent interruption of schooling due to financial hardship.

### 4.3 Examinations
- Registration fees for eligible WAEC and NECO candidates.
- Fees for other recognised examinations where the need and cost can be verified.
- Essential examination-related costs where these are necessary and within programme capacity.

### 4.4 Tertiary admission pathway
- JAMB/UTME registration for eligible candidates.
- Guidance on registration requirements, examination procedures and legitimate admission information.
- Referrals to scholarships, bursaries and other education-support opportunities.

JAMB/UTME support is intended for eligible candidates seeking tertiary admission; it is not a general school-fee benefit. The programme does not guarantee examination success, admission or scholarship awards.

## 5. How a student is identified

Students may be brought to the programme's attention by:

- A volunteer who knows the family or neighbourhood.
- A parent or legal guardian.
- A school representative, subject to appropriate school procedures.
- A community or neighbourhood association.
- A church, mosque or other faith-based organisation.
- An NGO, charity or community-based organisation.
- A trusted individual who can explain the concern and, where appropriate, help the family make contact.

A referral is only a request for consideration. It is not confirmation that support will be provided.

## 6. Assessment and verification

To protect students, families, volunteers and supporters, every request should be assessed respectfully and consistently.

The process should include:

1. **Initial contact:** Explain the programme and obtain the parent or guardian's agreement before collecting information about a child.
2. **Understand the need:** Establish what expense is outstanding, why it matters, the deadline and what may happen if it is not met.
3. **Confirm the education context:** With appropriate consent, confirm the student's enrolment or examination eligibility through the school or relevant official process.
4. **Verify the amount:** Request an official fee schedule, invoice, payment instruction or other reliable evidence. Do not rely solely on an informal verbal estimate.
5. **Consider other support:** Ask whether the school has a welfare arrangement, fee extension, bursary or other legitimate assistance that may help.
6. **Assess priority:** Consider urgency, the likely educational consequence, the household's circumstances and the resources available.
7. **Record the decision:** Record whether the request is approved, deferred, referred or declined, with a brief reason and without unnecessary personal details.

Verification must be proportionate. Volunteers should not conduct intrusive investigations, threaten families or demand humiliating proof of poverty.

## 7. Prioritisation

When resources are insufficient to meet every request, the programme should consider:

- Whether the student is at immediate risk of missing an examination or being unable to continue school.
- Whether the expense is essential, reasonable and verifiable.
- The urgency and deadline.
- The seriousness of the household's financial difficulty.
- Whether other support is available.
- Whether a smaller intervention could prevent a major interruption.
- Whether the programme can meet the need fairly without making commitments it cannot sustain.

Priority decisions should be documented. Personal connections, religion, ethnicity, political affiliation, social status or a supporter's preference must not unfairly determine who receives help.

## 8. Sponsorship and payment arrangements

Support may come from individuals, families, community groups, religious organisations, businesses, NGOs, charities and philanthropists who freely choose to participate.

Possible sponsorship arrangements include:

- Sponsoring one pupil's essential learning materials.
- Supporting one student's verified school expense.
- Sponsoring an eligible WAEC or NECO candidate's registration.
- Supporting an eligible JAMB/UTME candidate's registration.
- Contributing to a pooled education-support fund, if and when the initiative establishes appropriate financial controls.

Where practical, payment should be made directly to the school or authorised examination registration channel against verified payment instructions. The programme should issue or retain appropriate evidence of payment.

No volunteer may promise assistance before approval and confirmation of available funds. No volunteer may charge a beneficiary a fee, commission or personal payment for referral, assessment or access to assistance.

Any pooled funds must be handled transparently, with clear authorisation, records, receipts and periodic reporting. The initiative should not collect or hold money informally in a volunteer's personal account without an agreed and accountable process.

## 9. Working with schools and community organisations

The programme may invite public schools, parent associations, community leaders, religious bodies, NGOs and other organisations to help identify needs and connect families with support.

Such cooperation must be voluntary and based on clear agreement. An organisation must not be described publicly as a partner, sponsor or endorser unless it has expressly agreed.

The programme is independent and must not imply that it represents or is authorised by a school, government agency, examination body or religious institution.

Assistance must be non-discriminatory. No child or family may be required to attend worship, join a religious organisation, change belief, support a political party or participate in any unrelated activity to receive consideration.

## 10. Child safeguarding, privacy and dignity

The safety and dignity of children are central to this programme.

- Communicate with children through appropriate, transparent safeguarding arrangements and involve their parent or legal guardian.
- Do not arrange private or unsupervised meetings between a volunteer and a child.
- Do not publish a child's name, address, school identity, examination number, academic records, family hardship or photograph without appropriate informed consent and safeguarding review.
- Collect only information necessary to assess and deliver support.
- Do not request or retain passwords, one-time passcodes, bank PINs, BVN, NIN or other sensitive identifiers.
- Do not ask families to send sensitive documents into public WhatsApp groups or social media channels.
- Store any necessary records securely and restrict access to authorised programme personnel.
- Do not use a child's hardship as publicity, pressure or a fundraising spectacle.
- Treat every child and family respectfully, regardless of background, religion, disability, gender or family circumstances.

Where a safeguarding concern arises, volunteers should not investigate it themselves. They should follow appropriate child-protection procedures and refer the concern to a competent safeguarding authority or service.

## 11. Volunteer responsibilities

Volunteers are expected to:

- Act honestly, respectfully and without discrimination.
- Explain that assistance depends on verification, available resources and approval.
- Obtain appropriate consent before contacting schools or handling personal information.
- Avoid conflicts of interest and declare any personal relationship that could affect a decision.
- Never solicit money or gifts from beneficiaries.
- Never alter receipts, fee notices or student information.
- Keep beneficiary information confidential.
- Report concerns, errors or suspected misuse through the initiative's agreed accountability process.
- Follow up appropriately to establish whether approved support reached the intended student.

Volunteers contribute time, skills, information, referrals and practical assistance. They are not expected to personally finance every case.

## 12. Accountability and impact measurement

The programme should measure actual support and educational outcomes, not simply the number of appeals or messages circulated.

Subject to privacy safeguards, records may include:

- Number of requests received.
- Number of requests assessed and verified.
- Number of students supported.
- Type and value of assistance provided.
- Number of examination registrations supported.
- Number of students confirmed to have continued schooling or sat the relevant examination.
- Number of referrals made to scholarships or other support, recorded separately from assistance actually received.
- Amount received and spent, with appropriate supporting records.

Reports should distinguish clearly between:
- **Support provided:** assistance actually paid or delivered.
- **Referral made:** a student was connected to another organisation or opportunity.
- **Outcome confirmed:** there is reliable confirmation that the intended educational step occurred.

Do not report a referral as a successful award, a payment as an examination result, or an intention as a completed outcome. Public reports should use aggregated or anonymised information.

## 13. Implementation approach

The programme should grow gradually and responsibly.

### Stage 1: Establish the process
- Approve this programme framework.
- Identify a small number of responsible volunteers.
- Agree on a simple, confidential request and verification process.
- Establish basic financial authorisation and record-keeping arrangements before receiving pooled funds.

### Stage 2: Begin with manageable cases
- Identify genuine needs within volunteers' immediate families and neighbourhoods.
- Work with willing schools and community contacts.
- Support a small number of verified cases within confirmed resources.
- Keep evidence of payments and delivery.

### Stage 3: Build a trusted support network
- Invite individuals, faith-based organisations, community groups, businesses and charities to participate.
- Share anonymised impact reports.
- Seek recurring sponsors only after the programme can demonstrate sound accountability.

### Stage 4: Review and expand
- Review what worked, what did not and whether students benefited.
- Improve verification, safeguarding and financial controls.
- Expand to additional communities only when volunteer capacity and resources allow.

## 14. Important limitations

EVERY NEIGHBOUR COUNTS:

- Does not guarantee that every request will receive funding.
- Does not replace government education programmes, school welfare systems, scholarships or official examination bodies.
- Does not issue admission letters, examination credentials or school certificates.
- Does not guarantee examination results, admission or employment.
- Does not claim authority to waive school charges or change examination rules.
- Does not endorse unofficial agents or payment channels.

All fees, deadlines, eligibility rules and payment instructions must be checked against the relevant school or official examination body at the time of assistance.

## 15. Guiding commitment

We believe that helping one child remain in school can strengthen a family and contribute to the future of an entire community.

We will begin with needs within reach, act within our means, protect the dignity of every child, account for every contribution and report only what we can verify.
