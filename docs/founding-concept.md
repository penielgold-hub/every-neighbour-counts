# EVERY NEIGHBOUR COUNTS
## Founding Concept and Programme Framework

**Tagline:** Real People. Real Help. Measurable Impact.

**Guiding motto:** Your family first. Your neighbour next. Nobody left behind.

**Programme type:** Grassroots, volunteer-led community assistance initiative.

**Initial communication platform:** WhatsApp.

---

## 1. Introduction

Every Neighbour Counts is a grassroots community-support initiative founded on a simple but powerful belief: everyone can make a difference in the life of another person, beginning with those closest to them.

Across our communities, there are elderly people, widows, persons with disabilities, unemployed individuals and struggling households who need assistance. Some need financial support. Others need access to information, healthcare, education, digital services, employment opportunities or simply someone willing to help them navigate everyday challenges.

Many of these people live within our immediate families and neighbourhoods.

We believe meaningful community development should begin where we live, with the people we know and the needs we can understand.

## 2. Vision

To build communities where individuals take responsibility for helping vulnerable people within their immediate reach, creating a growing network of practical assistance, compassion and measurable social impact.

## 3. Mission

To empower individuals to identify vulnerable people within their families and neighbourhoods, understand their needs, provide practical assistance where possible and connect them with legitimate sources of support and opportunities.

## 4. Our Founding Philosophy

**Start with your family. Reach your neighbour. Extend kindness to the community.**

We do not need to wait for government, large organisations or wealthy philanthropists before making a difference.

Every individual has something valuable to contribute: time, knowledge, skills, resources, connections or encouragement.

Our initiative encourages people to begin with what they have and whom they can reach.

We believe that a person who helps one vulnerable neighbour has made a meaningful contribution to society.

## 5. Our Core Objectives

1. Encourage individuals to identify vulnerable members of their immediate families and neighbourhoods.
2. Promote practical assistance rather than promises.
3. Connect vulnerable people to legitimate government assistance, charitable organisations and available social programmes.
4. Improve access to information, digital services, education, healthcare and employment opportunities.
5. Develop a volunteer network through WhatsApp and other accessible communication channels.
6. Document activities, contributions and outcomes transparently.
7. Build partnerships with organisations that can help address identified community needs.

## 6. Areas of Intervention

### A. Elderly assistance
- Digital literacy and access to essential services.
- Assistance with everyday errands.
- Connecting elderly people with appropriate welfare services.

### B. Support for vulnerable households
- Identifying food and essential-needs assistance.
- Connecting households with legitimate welfare programmes.
- Helping people access available community support.

### C. Employment and skills opportunities
- Connecting unemployed people with genuine opportunities.
- Sharing verified training programmes.
- Encouraging skills development and digital inclusion.

### D. Persons with disabilities
- Identifying accessibility challenges.
- Connecting individuals with relevant support services.
- Promoting inclusion and dignity.

### E. Public information and social assistance
- Explaining government intervention programmes.
- Helping people understand eligibility and application procedures.
- Providing guidance on avoiding fraud and exploitation.

## 7. Our Volunteer Model

Every volunteer begins with their immediate environment.

The expected approach is:

1. Identify someone who may need help.
2. Understand the person's actual circumstances.
3. Determine what assistance is realistically available.
4. Provide practical help or make a legitimate referral.
5. Follow up to establish whether the assistance made a difference.
6. Encourage another individual to participate.

Volunteers are not expected to finance every problem they encounter. Their contribution may be time, knowledge, guidance, skills or access to useful contacts.

## 8. Communication and Community Mobilisation

WhatsApp will serve as our initial communication and education platform.

It will support:
- Educational broadcasts.
- Volunteer coordination.
- Sharing verified opportunities.
- Community assistance requests.
- Awareness campaigns.
- Reporting activities and outcomes.

Participation will be voluntary. Beneficiaries' privacy and dignity must always be respected.

### 8.1 Community and faith-based support partners

We recognise that trusted institutions and groups already serve people within local communities. Our support network may include:

- Churches, mosques and other religious or faith-based organisations.
- Religious welfare committees, outreach teams and community volunteers.
- Neighbourhood associations and community groups.
- Traditional and local leaders.
- NGOs, charities and community-based organisations.
- Government social-support agencies.
- Businesses, professional groups and individual philanthropists.

Faith-based organisations can help identify needs, share verified information, provide volunteers, offer suitable meeting spaces, make referrals and, where they choose and are able, contribute practical or material support.

**The initiative is open to everyone, regardless of religion or belief.** Assistance must never be conditional on religious affiliation, worship, conversion, membership or participation in religious activities. No person should be excluded or treated differently because they belong to another faith or have no religious affiliation.

Partnerships must be voluntary, transparent and based on the organisation's actual capacity. We will not imply that any religious body or institution has endorsed or partnered with the initiative until it has expressly agreed.

## 9. Accountability and Measurable Impact

Our success will not be measured by followers, messages circulated or promises made.

We will measure actual results.

Examples of impact indicators include:

- Number of vulnerable individuals genuinely assisted.
- Number of households receiving practical support.
- Number of people connected to legitimate assistance programmes.
- Number of employment or training referrals resulting in participation.
- Number of volunteers actively contributing.
- Number of identified problems successfully addressed.
- Number and type of community or faith-based organisations that have agreed to support specific activities.

We will distinguish between referrals made, assistance delivered and outcomes independently confirmed.

No beneficiary's private information will be published without appropriate consent.

## 10. Governance and Integrity

Every Neighbour Counts will operate on principles of:

- Compassion.
- Honesty.
- Transparency.
- Accountability.
- Non-discrimination.
- Respect for human dignity.
- Non-partisanship and non-sectarianism.
- Protection of personal information.

We will not promise assistance that we cannot deliver, misrepresent our authority or exploit vulnerable people.

We will not collect money from beneficiaries in exchange for access to assistance.

We will verify information before sharing it and clearly distinguish our independent volunteer role from the authority of government agencies, religious bodies and partner organisations.

## 11. Safeguarding and Privacy

Volunteers must:
- Obtain consent before assisting or sharing someone's story.
- Collect only the minimum information needed for a specific activity.
- Keep personal information secure and restrict access.
- Never publish NINs, BVNs, bank details, passwords, OTPs or private contact details.
- Avoid photographing or identifying beneficiaries without informed consent.
- Respect a person's right to decline assistance.
- Refer serious health, protection or safeguarding concerns to appropriate qualified services.

Public reports should use aggregated or anonymised information wherever possible.

## 12. Relationship with Government and Other Assistance Programmes

The initiative may help people understand and access legitimate government programmes, NGO services, charitable support and other opportunities.

For example, the Federal Government's NERSI ₦50,000 relief intervention may be documented as a specific public-information and referral campaign.

However, no single government programme defines the initiative.

Every Neighbour Counts is independent. It is not a government agency, beneficiary-selection authority or payment provider. Volunteers cannot add people to official beneficiary lists, guarantee eligibility or promise payment.

Programme information must be checked against current official sources before being shared.

## 13. Growth Strategy

### Phase 1: Foundation
Establish the initiative, document its objectives, create the GitHub repository and prepare volunteer educational materials.

### Phase 2: Family and neighbourhood participation
Encourage volunteers to identify and assist vulnerable people within their immediate surroundings.

### Phase 3: Community coordination
Build a network of volunteers who can share information, identify needs and coordinate legitimate assistance, including through willing community and faith-based partners.

### Phase 4: Institutional partnerships
Engage NGOs, charities, religious organisations, government agencies, businesses and other organisations for collaboration.

### Phase 5: Impact reporting and expansion
Publish periodic reports documenting activities, verified outcomes, lessons and opportunities for improvement.

## 14. The Role of the Founder

The founder's responsibility is to establish the vision, provide direction, coordinate the initiative's development, promote transparency and encourage participation.

The founder is not expected to personally finance every intervention.

The long-term objective is to build a sustainable network in which individuals and organisations contribute according to their abilities and available resources.

## 15. Our Founding Declaration

We recognise that meaningful change does not always begin with large donations, government programmes or major institutions.

Sometimes it begins with a telephone call to an elderly parent.

Sometimes it begins with visiting a struggling neighbour.

Sometimes it begins with helping someone access an opportunity they did not know existed.

Sometimes it begins when a local church, mosque, community group or neighbour joins hands to support someone in need.

We believe that every person has the capacity to make a positive difference within their immediate environment.

We therefore commit ourselves to practical action, responsible assistance and measurable results.

**We will not measure our success by how loudly we speak about helping people, but by the difference our actions make in their lives.**
